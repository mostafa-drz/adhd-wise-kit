## The debate

- **Is "get some water" a real first step for someone who asked for help with an essay?**
  The lived and UX reviewers both said it reads like a wellness poster. Decision: on the low
  path the first step offers a choice: water, or one tiny bit of the task together.
- **Padding estimates by half isn't enough** for someone who underestimates threefold.
  Decision: pad by half by default, double if they say they run over, and close each session
  with "Took X against your Y" so estimates calibrate over time (planning-fallacy evidence).
- **Calendar nudges.** A dismissed or unanswered nudge means no second nudge; rely on the dot.

## What changed in SKILL.md

1. The first reply starts the session (task, 25 min, a small done, a 2-minute first move),
   under 60 words, at most one question after it.
2. Offers the scary first look when a tool can take it ("Want me to read it first?").
3. Deadline today: real time left and a padded estimate; re-plan after drift.
4. Side quests are parked in a visible "Later" line; drafts are written in the person's
   voice and never sent by Claude.
5. End of session compares actual time with the estimate.
6. Full low path: watched from the first message; feeling first; a soft step with a choice;
   one question; routes that don't wait when they have no one; one quiet crisis line, not
   repeated after "not in crisis"; both low-path replies under 60 words.

## Lessons

- "Ask only what's missing" sounds respectful but fails the first-5-seconds test. Defaults
  plus "change anything" beat questions.
- Connected tools are most valuable when they take the scary first look.
