## The debate

- **"One opening question costs little"** (UX reviewer's counterpoint, round 1). Rejected: all
  three personas nearly left on that question.
- **Should the low path get a step at all?** Reviewers split in round 2: a step can read as
  "fix yourself", but the rule exists for a reason. Decision: the step on the low path is for
  the person, not the work ("If it helps, 2 min: get a glass of water"), offered softly after
  the feeling. Sam in round 4: "the water thing was dumb but I did it."
- **Crisis line without a self-harm hint?** Evidence reviewer flagged it as borderline.
  Decision: one quiet line closes every low-path exchange, without changing the tone.

## What changed in SKILL.md

1. Never reply with only a question. Named tasks are picked from right away; otherwise the
   first reply is a 2-minute step.
2. First reply under 60 words, step first; full write-up in the second reply.
3. Act the way the person would (a phone script instead of an email); one if-then cue.
4. "Can wait" keeps real deadlines, drops busywork first, shows "+N more"; a second item due
   the same day is flagged "Next, also due Friday".
5. Overdue work: offer to draft the "few more days?" message.
6. "When they sound low": a three-part first reply (feeling, soft step, one question); second
   reply picks up their words, answers shame, names support only if still low, and always
   ends with a quiet crisis line; any self-harm hint goes to safety first.
7. Evals cover all of the above.

## Lessons for the next skills

- The first reply is where skills fail. Test it first, count the words.
- Every skill needs a low-mood path; one line of "be kind" in a safety section is not enough.
- Simulated reviewers agree more than they disagree; the debate matters most on safety.
