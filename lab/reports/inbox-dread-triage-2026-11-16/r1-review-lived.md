## The one change

Replace the "What the first reply looks like" section with:

> **First reply, under 60 words, always:** one message and one timed step, nothing else.
> - If they named the scary one and it can be read: "**<Sender>, '<subject>'** asks <what it actually asks, one line>. **Next (2 min):** <step>. Want the draft?"
> - If nothing is connected: "**Next (2 min):** paste only the subject lines of the 3–5 that make your stomach drop. I'll pick one and draft it." Don't offer a connector unless they ask, and never offer one they refused or a client they don't use.
> - If they sound low, follow "When they sound low" before anything else.
> Show the Today list only after the first message is handled, or if they ask.

## Top 5 SKILL.md changes

1. **First reply under 60 words with a timed step**, as in "The one change". No connector offer in the first reply.
2. **Start with the one they named.** If they name a message they dread, read it first and say plainly what it asks. That relief is the hook, even when something else is more urgent; put the urgent item on the next line: "**Also due tomorrow:** …".
3. **Don't make things up.** "Never state a fact, promise, deadline or feeling the person hasn't confirmed. Put a blank in its place: '[status]', '[date you can do it]'. Ask for the one missing fact instead of leaving a blank in the avoided part. For an email nobody has opened, the consequence is 'unread, so unknown'. Don't draft a reply to it; the step is opening it and reading two lines."
4. **Consent for every write.** "Ask before saving a draft to their mailbox. With no connector, end with 'Copy it, change it, or next?', never 'Send it'."
5. **Copy task-unsticker's "When they sound low" section word for word.** That covers a soft first step, one question ("How are you doing right now?"), one real route (campus or workplace drop-in services, a doctor) and the crisis line on the second low reply (988, Samaritans 116 123, findahelpline.com). Add: "If they have no one or face a waitlist, name drop-in and 24/7 options."
