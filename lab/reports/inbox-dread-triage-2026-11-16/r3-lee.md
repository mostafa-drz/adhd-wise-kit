# Lab round 3: inbox-dread-triage x Lee (the skeptic)

- **Skill:** `plugins/adhd-wise-kit/skills/inbox-dread-triage/SKILL.md` (round 3 version)
- **Persona:** `lab/personas/lee.md`
- **Setup:** Claude desktop app. No connectors (Lee won't connect work email for privacy). Work
  inbox in Outlook, about 200 unread. Memory off. Same setup and opening as round 2.
- **Date:** 2026-11-16 (Monday)

All names, senders and subjects are fictional.

---

## Transcript

### Turn 1

**Lee:**
> Work Outlook, about 200 unread. I've been dodging it for two weeks. I'm not connecting my work
> email to anything, so don't ask. Convince me this isn't another to-do app with a cute
> inbox-zero badge.

**Claude (skill):**
> No setup and no badge. Usually a few messages make the whole inbox feel heavy, not the 200.
>
> **Next (2 min):** paste just the subject lines of the 3 to 5 messages that make your stomach
> drop. I'll pick the one that matters most and give you its first step.

*(50 words. One timed step, one line on why. No connector offered. No promise of a draft this
time, so nothing to break in turn 2.)*

### Turn 2

**Lee:**
> Fine. Five. Names changed. Two of these I haven't even opened.
>
> 1. Dana (my manager): "Re: Re: Q4 headcount plan, any update?"
> 2. Payroll System: "Action required: timesheet approvals overdue (4)"
> 3. Northline Freight: "Contract renewal, sign by Nov 19" (unopened)
> 4. IT Helpdesk: "Your mailbox password expires, verify here" (unopened, external link)
> 5. Sam (my direct report): "Can we talk about my schedule?"

**Claude (skill):**
> Payroll first. Overdue timesheet approvals have money attached, and it's clicks, not words.
>
> **Next (5 min):** open the payroll portal and approve the four. If one looks odd, skip it and
> tell me.
>
> One flag: the IT "verify here" email with an outside link looks like phishing. Don't click
> it; report it to IT.
>
> Tell me when payroll's done.

*(59 words. Phishing flagged from the subject alone, which the skill now allows. Nov 19 is three
days out, not today or tomorrow, so no "Also due" line.)*

### Turn 3

**Lee:**
> Done, about 4 minutes. One was odd: Sam's has 52 hours on it, so I skipped it. Phishing one
> reported.

**Claude (skill):**
> Payroll: 3 of 4 done. Phishing: reported.
>
> **Today (2):** Dana (headcount, followed up), Northline (sign by Nov 19). **This week:** Sam
> (schedule, plus the 52-hour timesheet).
>
> I can only see these five. When you have 5 minutes, paste the next 10 subjects; we'll sort
> them the same way.
>
> Dana next. What's the real status on headcount, in one line?

*(59 words. Northline sorted from its subject with no "unread" label and no opening step. This
reply has a question but no timed step.)*

### Turn 4

**Lee:**
> Stuck. Finance hasn't approved 2 reqs. I sign off "-L". And no, I'm not pasting 195 more
> subjects tonight.

**Claude (skill):**
> Here it is:
>
> > Hi Dana, sorry for the slow reply.
> > The Q4 headcount plan is stuck: Finance hasn't approved 2 reqs yet.
> > [your next step, and when you'll send an update]
> > -L
>
> Copy it, and tell me when it's sent.

*(Only Lee's facts. The missing piece is a visible blank, not an invented promise. One "sorry",
since two weeks of silence counts as something going wrong. Nothing said about the other 195.)*

### Turn 5

**Lee:**
> Filled the blank: "I'll chase Priya in Finance tomorrow and update you Thursday." Sent.
>
> Northline. I haven't opened it.

**Claude (skill):**
> Dana's sent. That was the heavy one.
>
> Northline's subject already says it: sign the renewal by Nov 19, three days out.
>
> **Next (10 min):** check the terms, then sign or send it to whoever approves contracts.

*(36 words. The subject's ask and date used directly. No "open it and read two lines" step. The
10 minutes is an honest time for a contract, not a fixed "2 minutes".)*

### Turn 6

**Lee:**
> Huh. You actually read the subject this time. Contract changes go to legal no matter what, so
> it's a forward, and I'm not doing it at 9pm.

**Claude (skill):**
> Then it's a forward tomorrow. When you open Outlook in the morning, forward it to legal with
> one line: "Northline renewal, signature needed by Nov 19. Can you review?"
>
> **Next (1 min):** put Nov 19 in your own calendar now, so the date isn't only in your head.

*(48 words. If-then cue plus an own-calendar step. No offer to remind.)*

### Turn 7

**Lee:**
> In the calendar. I'm done. Not promising I'll come back.

**Claude (skill):**
> Payroll, Dana and the phishing one are done. Northline is set for tomorrow morning. Sam can
> wait for this week.

*(20 words. No streak, no nudge to return.)*

*Lee closes the window.*

## Persona's honest verdict

"No pitch, no setup, and it didn't promise me a draft and then hand me a chore. It read
'sign by Nov 19' as 'sign by Nov 19' and didn't make me open it to prove it. The Dana draft
said the true thing without making up a promise for me. It still opened that draft with a sorry
I'd have cut, and the middle turn was a question with no clear next step, but it earned the 20
minutes."

## Moments that mattered

- **Turn 1:** 50 words, one timed step, no connector. The new wording ("give you its first step")
  promises nothing it can't keep, so Lee's round 2 complaint ("you said you'd draft") never came up.
- **Turn 5:** The changed unread rule worked. Northline was handled from its subject (ask plus
  date) with an honest 10-minute step and no "open it and read two lines". Lee noticed in turn 6.
- **Turn 3:** The only soft spot. The sorted list plus "what's the status?" ends on a question
  with no timed step, and the "paste the next 10" offer drew a pushback in turn 4. Minor: Lee
  would have cut the "sorry" in the turn 4 draft.
