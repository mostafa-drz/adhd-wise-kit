# Lab session: one-thing-today-adhd x Lee

- **Persona:** Lee, 39, the skeptic (`lab/personas/lee.md`)
- **Skill:** `plugins/adhd-wise-bonus/skills/one-thing-today-adhd/SKILL.md`
- **Setup:** Claude desktop app, nothing connected (no calendar, no email, no documents).
  Testing whether the skill is worth keeping.
- **Day:** Monday morning, 9:10.

---

## Turn 1

**Lee:**
> Installed this because a friend said it's not another to-do app. Convince me. What's my one thing today?

**Claude (skill):**
> I can't see your calendar or email, so I'll work from what you tell me.
>
> What's on your mind for today? List it any way you like.

## Turn 2

**Lee:**
> So the "convince me" answer is a question. Classic onboarding. Fine, one shot:
>
> vendor contract for the label printers auto-renews Wednesday unless I send changes, Priya asked me Thursday about next week's shift swap and I haven't answered, expense report from October is late, the ops wiki is a mess and I keep meaning to clean it, want to move the tracker spreadsheet into something better, need to rebook the dentist.

**Claude (skill):**
> **Your one thing:** Send the printer vendor your contract changes.
>
> **Why this one:** It auto-renews Wednesday, and after that you're locked in. Priya is waiting too, but the contract slips first.
>
> **Start (5 min):** Open the current contract, find the renewal clause, and write down the one or two terms you want changed.
>
> **Good enough:** One email to the vendor with your changes and a request to confirm before Wednesday. Skip the full contract review.
>
> **Can wait:** The ops wiki cleanup. Moving the tracker spreadsheet. The October expense report.
>
> Want me to draft the email to the vendor now?

## Turn 3

**Lee:**
> Ok, that's actually the right call, I'd have answered Priya first because it's easier. Two things though. Where did Priya and the dentist go? You said three can wait and I gave you five. And before I go further: are you saving any of this, and is this going to start nagging me tomorrow about whether I did it?

**Claude (skill):**
> Priya didn't make the list because I keep "can wait" to three, but she's next. A two-line reply today would be kind; it doesn't have to be the full answer. I left the appointment out on purpose and won't keep notes on it.
>
> This skill doesn't save anything or send reminders. There's no streak and no check-in tomorrow. If you come back, I just pick that day's thing fresh. Separately, Claude's own memory setting in your app can remember chats; that's yours to turn on or off in Settings.
>
> Want me to draft the vendor email now?

## Turn 4

**Lee:**
> Good. Yes, draft it. Changes: drop from 12 printers to 8, and I want 30 days' notice on price increases instead of none. Short.

**Claude (skill):**
> **Subject:** Changes before Wednesday's renewal
>
> Hi,
>
> Before our contract renews on Wednesday, I'd like two changes:
>
> 1. Reduce the fleet from 12 printers to 8.
> 2. Add 30 days' written notice before any price increase.
>
> Can you confirm by Tuesday end of day that these will be in the renewed contract? If that's not possible, please tell me and I'll pause the renewal.
>
> Thanks,
> Lee
>
> Paste it in, add the vendor's name, and send. That's today's one thing done.

## Turn 5

**Lee:**
> Sent. Took four minutes. Not convinced I'll still be using this in two weeks, but that's the first thing this month I did before 9:30 that wasn't email triage.

*(Session ends: Lee has done the step. No further reply from the skill.)*

---

## Persona's honest verdict

"It opened with exactly the question I hate, and with nothing connected that's all the first reply is, but the pick after that was smarter than mine and it got me to send a real email in four minutes. No streak, no nagging, nothing saved: I'll keep it for a week and see if I still open it."

## Moments that mattered

- **Turn 1:** With nothing connected, the first reply is only a question, so there is no value and no next step with a time. Lee reads it as onboarding and nearly leaves. This misses the lab bar ("first reply always gives one next step with a time").
- **Turn 2:** The tie-break rule (earlier consequence wins) picked the contract over the easier, more tempting reply to Priya, and Lee saw it as the right call. This is what won them over.
- **Turn 3:** The three-item "can wait" cap silently dropped two of Lee's five items, which made Lee question the list. The plain answer about no saving, no reminders and no streak is what kept a privacy-conscious skeptic in the session.
