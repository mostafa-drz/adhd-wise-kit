## Evidence for scores below 4

- **Ruth, 1 (3):** The first reply runs about 90 words on a phone at 9pm, and the step with a
  time comes third, after "Your one thing" and "Why". Ruth: "Long, but I can see the bold bits."
  The skill caps only the nothing-named reply at 60 words.
- **Sam, 1 (2):** The first reply has no step and no time. The low path drops it entirely and
  ends with a vague offer ("one tiny step"), so Sam needs two more turns to ask for it ("can we
  just do the essay thing, the tiny step"). That breaks the skill's own rule, "every first reply
  gives a step with a time".

## Verdict

**Fail.** Criterion 1 averages 3.33, below the bar of 4, and two of three first replies (Ruth
about 90 words, Sam no step) miss the README's "one next step with a time, under 60 words".

## The one change

Replace this line in `SKILL.md`:

> Rules for every reply: the one thing first, no more than three "can wait" items, no shame,
> plain words. **Never reply with only a question:** every first reply gives a step with a time.

with:

> Rules for every reply: no more than three "can wait" items, no shame, plain words.
> **The first reply is under 60 words and opens with the step and its time**, for example
> "**Start (5 min):** take the renewal letter out of the drawer and circle the price." In a
> first reply use only **Start**, **Your one thing** (one line) and the offer to do it; give
> Why, Good enough and Can wait in the next reply or when asked. When they sound low, answer
> the feeling first, then end with the step as a soft option: "If it helps, one 2-minute step:
> <step>. No promise needed."

## Strongest point against my verdict

The low path may rightly be exempt from the step-with-a-time bar: putting any task in front of
someone who just said "what's the point" can read as not listening, and Sam stayed precisely
because the first reply had no task.
