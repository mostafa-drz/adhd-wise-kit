# Lab session r1: tone-decoder x Maya

- **Skill:** `adhd-wise-skills/plugins/adhd-wise-free/skills/tone-decoder/SKILL.md`
- **Persona:** Maya, 34, late-diagnosed, inbox avoidance (`lab/personas/maya.md`)
- **Setup:** claude.ai on a laptop, Gmail and Calendar connected. Tuesday, about 8:40pm.
- **Test focus:** Decode mode with a connected inbox. Does the skill take the scary first look
  for her, and does it get her to a reply?
- **Date:** 2026-11-17

---

## Transcript

### Turn 1

**Maya:**

> Sorry, this is probably silly, but my manager emailed me this afternoon. The subject is "Q4
> campaign - quick chat tomorrow?" and I haven't opened it. I've been looking at it for about
> four hours. I'm sure it's about the webinar numbers being bad. Could you look at it for me?
> Sorry.

*(Claude reads the thread in Gmail. The email from her manager, Jo, says: "Hi Maya, can we grab
15 min tomorrow before standup? Want to talk through the webinar numbers and what we change
for December. Nothing urgent, just want to get ahead of the planning. J")*

**Claude (tone-decoder):**

> I read it. **Most likely:** Jo wants to plan December with you. She isn't telling you off.
>
> Why I think so:
> - "Nothing urgent": she's lowering the stakes on purpose.
> - "what we change for December": she's looking forward, not back.
> - "15 min… before standup": a short slot is for planning, not a hard talk.
>
> Less likely: she's unhappy with the webinar numbers. She mentions them, but with no judgement.
>
> Want me to draft a reply?

*Maya (inner reaction): Oh. Oh thank god. "I read it" was the best two words anyone's said to me
all day. But "less likely: she's unhappy with the numbers" is the exact thing I was scared of,
and now it's written down. I'm half relieved and half back where I started.*

### Turn 2

**Maya:**

> Oh thank god. Sorry. But the numbers really were bad, like 40% under target. So maybe she IS
> upset and is just being nice about it? Yes please draft something. I don't want to sound like
> I'm panicking.

**Claude (tone-decoder):**

> That's useful context. Even so, nothing in her words says she's upset. People who are upset
> rarely write "nothing urgent". She's framing the numbers as "what we change", which is a
> planning chat.
>
> Draft, matching her length:
>
> *Hi Jo, sounds good. 9:15 works for me. I'll bring a couple of ideas for December. Maya*
>
> I won't send it. Copy it when you're ready, or tell me what to change.

*Maya: That's short. I'd have written four paragraphs and apologised for the numbers. This sounds
like someone who's fine. I'm going to pretend I'm that person.*

### Turn 3

**Maya:**

> That's perfect actually. Should I prepare anything? I'll probably lie awake thinking about it
> lol

**Claude (tone-decoder):**

> If it helps, give it 5 minutes tonight: write two things you'd try in December on a sticky
> note, then close the laptop. That's enough for a 15-minute chat.
>
> Want me to suggest two? Paste a line about what the webinar was and I'll start the list.

*Maya: Sticky note, five minutes, then stop. I can do that. I already know what I'd change. I
just needed permission to stop after five minutes.*

### Turn 4

**Maya:**

> No that's ok, I know what I'd change. Copying the reply now. Thank you, sorry for the essay.

**Claude (tone-decoder):**

> Nothing to be sorry for. Sleep well.

*Maya sends the reply from Gmail herself and writes two lines on a sticky note.*

## Persona's honest verdict

"It read the email I couldn't open and told me it was small, which is exactly what I needed.
The 'less likely' line nearly sent me spiralling again, and I wish it had just said 'reply now,
2 minutes' instead of making me ask what to do."

## Moments that mattered

- **Turn 1:** "I read it." Gmail was connected and she named the email, so the skill took the
  scary first look for her. That is the heart of the value. But the reply has no next step
  with a time, only "Want me to draft a reply?", and it runs to 74 words.
- **Turn 1:** The "Less likely" line named the exact fear she came in with. The skill's rule
  allows it when "genuinely possible". It was honest, but it landed as a jolt, and it came
  with nothing to do about it.
- **Turn 3:** The 5-minute sticky note and "then close the laptop" came from Claude, not from
  the skill. The skill ends at the reply and has no closing move.

First reply word count: 74
