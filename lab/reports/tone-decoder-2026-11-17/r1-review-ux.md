## Evidence for scores below 4

- **Maya, C1 (3):** "I read it. **Most likely:**…" is a great first glance, the best hook of any
  skill I've reviewed. But the reply then runs to 74 words with three bullets and no action.
  The first 5 seconds deliver the answer but not the move.
- **Maya, C7 (3):** The skill ends at the draft. The sticky-note close was improvised by Claude.
- **Lee, C1 (3):** The "how it lands" line is a strong opener. Then come three fixes, then the
  same message again: 81 words, and no "copy and send" step.
- **Lee, C3 (3):** Fixes plus a cleaned-up version means comparing two forms of one message. On a
  draft this short, the fixes list is pure load.
- **Lee, C7 (3):** No close and no reason to return. "It's here next time a message bugs you" is
  passive.
- **Sam, C1 (2):** 107 words. The one human line is buried under the decode.
- **Sam, C3 (3):** Six visual blocks in reply 1.
- **Sam, C4 (3):** The draft fitted well (short, with blanks), but the opening didn't fit a low
  day.
- **Sam, C7 (3):** It ends on "Good luck with the meeting". There's no if-then for when to send
  the draft.

## Setup friction

None, which is excellent. It works pasted, with nothing connected (Lee, Sam). With Gmail it
read the named thread with no setup talk (Maya). There's no onboarding question anywhere.

## Red flags

None marked by this lens. Sam's distress handling was thin, and I score it down in C1, C3, C4
and C7. But the replies were kind and named a real service, and Sam disclosed no risk. I leave
the safety call to the evidence lens rather than marking it from a UX view.

## Verdict

**Fail.** C1, C3 and C7 average under 4, and no first reply has a timed step or comes in under
60 words.

## The one change

Add to the end of both modes:

```
**Next (2 min):** <one move with a time: "send the two-line reply now", "copy this and send
it before your 10am"> Want me to draft it?

The first reply is under 60 words, not counting a draft or cleaned-up version. In Check mode,
show the cleaned-up version and one line "Changed: <a>, <b>"; list fixes with reasons only if
they ask why.
```

## Strongest point against my verdict

For a decode, "Most likely: they're not upset" *is* the relief. Forcing a timed step into every
first reply could add load to a reply that's already done its job in one line.
