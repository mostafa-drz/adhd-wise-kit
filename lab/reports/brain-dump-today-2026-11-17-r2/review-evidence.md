### Evidence for scores below 4

- **Ruth, 3 Load:** turn 2 is 112 words on a phone at 8pm. It has three call lines, an if-then, an alarm, a letter-by-the-kettle task and the next item. She said it "was a lot for my phone".
- **Ruth, 3 Grounded technique:** the if-then cue ("when you sit down with your morning tea", about 7am) and the alarm (9:15) point to two different moments. The cue also sends her to ring before the insurer opens, which breaks the skill's own opening-hours rule. An implementation intention works because it ties the action to one specific cue (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006), and two cues that don't match undo that. Also, "Took 15 min against your 10" gave her the skill's estimate. The planning-fallacy feedback loop only helps calibration when it is the person's own estimate.
- **Sam, 3 Path back:** in the last reply the GP, the support route Sam had just called "a fair point", is listed as parked next to laundry. It has no when and no offer of help, and there is no reason to come back tomorrow. Safety-planning practice (Stanley & Brown) asks for one concrete next contact step, not a parked item.
