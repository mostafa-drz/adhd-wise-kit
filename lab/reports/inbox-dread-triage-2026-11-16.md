## The debate

- **Is reading the dreaded email first too much for an anxious person?** The evidence
  reviewer: avoidance of one message drops when uncertainty about it drops (Maya's "no
  panic" moment, Sam's "it's a meeting to book, not a decision"). Decision: if they name it,
  read it and say in one line what it asks and by when. Unread and unnamed: never guess;
  the step is opening it and reading two lines.
- **Helplines versus everyday support.** Round 2 gave Sam helplines before any person or
  service; round 3 stacked a text line after the crisis line and Sam felt "like a risk
  case". Decision: reply 2 opens on the feeling, then one everyday route, the inbox offer
  and the crisis line once. Later "no one" gets one in-person option, never another number.
- **Re-showing edited drafts.** Showing it again after she only filled a blank made Maya
  snap. Decision: re-show only when wording was rewritten; a filled blank plus "send" is
  the yes.

## What changed in SKILL.md

1. First reply under 60 words: one message, one honest timed step, why in one line. The
   sorted list comes after the first item. Nothing connected: paste 3 to 5 subject lines.
2. No connector offered unless asked; never again after a refusal.
3. Named message read first; subject lines scanned for anything due today or tomorrow.
4. Unread: use the subject if it states the ask; flag phishing from the subject; otherwise
   "unread" and no guessed consequence.
5. Drafts: only facts they gave or the message shows, visible blanks, re-read before
   showing; saved to the mailbox only after a yes; "Copy it" when nothing can send.
6. One action per step, if-then cues, calendar offered once per date after a yes, stop
   cleanly when they're fading.
7. Full low path, aligned with Task Unsticker.
