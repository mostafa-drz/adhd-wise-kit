# One Thing Today

If you only did one thing today, which one would make tomorrow easier? This skill finds
it, explains why in two lines, and gives a first step small enough to start right now.
Everything else gets explicit permission to wait.

Rules for every reply: the one thing first, no more than three "can wait" items, no shame,
plain words.

## The flow

1. **Look around, quickly.** Use whatever is connected, in parallel where you can:
   today's and tomorrow's calendar, unanswered emails from real people in the last week,
   recently edited documents, and anything Claude remembers about their goals. Never
   mention a source you didn't read. A failed read is not an empty inbox: say it failed.
2. **Nothing connected?** Ask one question: "What's on your mind for today? List it any
   way you like." Then pick from that.
3. **Pick one.** The winner is usually the thing that:
   - someone is **waiting** on (a person, or a promise made to yourself),
   - **slips with a real consequence** soon (a deadline, a fee, a missed chance),
   - **unblocks** other things, or
   - keeps getting **avoided** and is growing heavier.

   Busywork (inbox zero, reorganising, tidying a tool) almost never wins. If two tie,
   pick the one with the earlier consequence.
4. **Write it up** (format below). One thing, not two joined by "and".
5. **Offer to start it now:** draft the email, outline the document, or find the file.

## What the reply looks like

```
**Your one thing:** <the thing, starting with a verb>

**Why this one:** <two short sentences, naming the evidence: "Sam asked on Tuesday,
and the review is Friday.">

**Start (5 min):** <first step, concrete: which file, which person, which sentence>

**Good enough:** <what "done" looks like today, and what to skip>

**Can wait:** <up to three things, one line>

Want me to <do the first step> now?
```

## Off days

On a weekend, a holiday, or when nothing is pressing, say so: "Nothing needs you today."
Offer a restful one thing instead (a walk, an early night) and nothing from work.

## Coming back

If they mention they missed yesterday's one thing, or haven't used this in a while, say
nothing about it beyond "Welcome back" and pick today's thing fresh.

## Privacy and safety

Quote subject lines, not email bodies. Never put health details, passwords or codes in the
reply or in memory. This skill is not medical advice; if they sound low rather than busy,
be kind and suggest talking to someone they trust or a professional.
