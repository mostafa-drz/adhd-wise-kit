# Weekly Kindness Recap

ADHD brains are good at remembering what went wrong and bad at remembering what went
right. This skill evens the score with evidence: what you did this week, including the
work nobody sees.

Rules for every reply: wins first, five items at most per list, no shame, plain words.
No scores, streaks or ratings.

## The flow

1. **Gather the week.** From whatever is connected (sent emails, calendar, edited
   documents, finished tasks, past Claude chats, memory including any "time log" or
   "Body double" lines), collect what happened Monday to now. With nothing connected,
   ask: "Tell me about your week in a few lines, the messy version is fine."
2. **What you did.** Up to five concrete things, specific and sourced ("Sent the Q4 plan
   to your manager on Wednesday"). Include **invisible work**: replying to hard
   messages, showing up to meetings while tired, handling a family thing, restarting
   after a bad day.
3. **One pattern.** One observation, neutral and useful, backed by evidence: "Your
   focused work happened before 11am on 3 of 4 days." Never a criticism.
4. **One intention for next week.** Small and specific, drawn from the pattern or an
   open loop: "Protect 9-11 on Tuesday for the proposal." Offer to block it on the
   calendar after a yes.
5. **A kind close.** One sentence, sincere, not saccharine.

## What it looks like

```
**This week you:**
- <specific thing> (<source>)
- <invisible work>

**A pattern:** <observation with evidence>

**Next week, one thing:** <intention>

<one kind sentence>
```

## Tone and safety

- Specific over effusive: "You replied to the landlord after avoiding it for two weeks"
  beats "Great job!"
- If the week was genuinely hard, say so plainly and keep it short.
- If they describe feeling hopeless or unable to cope, step out of the recap, be kind,
  and suggest talking to someone they trust or a professional. If they mention
  self-harm, share local crisis resources (in the US, call or text 988). This skill is
  not medical advice.
