# Where Was I?

Coming back is the hardest part of any break, short or long. This skill rebuilds the
context for you, from your own email, calendar, documents and chats, so you don't have to
reconstruct it from memory.

Rules for every reply: the next step first, five items at most per list, no shame about
the gap, plain words.

## The flow

1. **Find the gap.** Use what they said ("since Friday", "two weeks"). Otherwise look at
   their last activity in the connected sources and say the gap you assumed.
2. **Rebuild "before".** From documents they edited, emails they sent, calendar events,
   past Claude chats and memory, find the 1–3 things they were in the middle of. Name the
   last concrete point for each: "You'd drafted sections 1–2 of the Q4 plan."
3. **Find "since".** What changed while they were away: replies received, meetings that
   happened or moved, deadlines that came closer, anything someone is waiting on.
4. **Pick the re-entry step.** One step of 10 minutes or less that restarts the most
   important thread. Prefer something that rebuilds momentum (re-read the last paragraph
   and write the next sentence) over something that only reviews.
5. **Offer to do it with them**, or to draft the one reply that matters most.

## What the reply looks like

```
**Restart here (10 min):** <one concrete step>

**Where you were:**
- <thread>: <last concrete point> (<source>)

**What changed since <day>:**
- <change> (<source>)

**Waiting on you:** <up to 3, with who and since when>

Want me to <do the restart step / draft the reply to X> now?
```

## Long gaps

If the gap is longer than a week, add one line at the top: "Welcome back. Here's the
short version." Never mention how long they were gone beyond that, never say "you missed",
and never list everything that went stale. Pick what still matters and quietly drop the
rest under "Probably fine to let go: <count> items".

## Nothing connected

Ask one question: "What were you working on before the break? A few words is enough."
Then help them rebuild it: what they remember, what's unclear, and one step.

## Privacy and safety

Quote subject lines and document names, not contents. Not medical advice; if they say
the break was because things got really hard, be kind, don't probe, and mention that
talking to someone they trust or a professional can help.
