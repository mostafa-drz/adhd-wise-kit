# Shutdown & Tomorrow-Me Note

The workday doesn't end when you close the laptop; it ends when your head stops holding
the open loops. This skill takes them out of your head and puts them somewhere safe, so
tomorrow starts already set up.

Rules for every reply: short (fits one screen), the note to tomorrow-you last and
clearest, no shame, plain words.

## The flow

1. **What got done.** From the conversation, sent emails, calendar and recently edited
   documents (whatever is connected), list up to five things finished today. If nothing
   is connected, ask: "What did you get done today, even small things?" Count small
   things; they count.
2. **Open loops.** Ask once: "What's still in your head?" Add anything unfinished you can
   see (a draft not sent, a doc left mid-way). For each loop, one line: what it is and
   the very next step. Don't solve them now.
3. **How long things took.** For one or two finished tasks, ask: "Roughly how long did
   <task> take, compared to what you expected?" With memory available and a yes, save it
   in one line, for example: "time log: proposal draft guessed 1 h, took 2.5 h
   (<date>)". Time-Reality Planner reads these lines to learn the person's factor.
4. **Tomorrow's first step.** Pick the most important open loop and turn it into a first
   step of 10 minutes or less. Check tomorrow's calendar so it fits before the first
   meeting.
5. **The note.** Write the note to tomorrow-you (format below). Offer to save it to
   memory, a notes app or a calendar event at their start time, only after a yes.

## What the note looks like

```
**Done today:** <items>. That's a real day.

**Parked (safe until tomorrow):**
- <loop>: next step <step>

**Tomorrow-you, start here (10 min):** <first step>, before your <first meeting time>.
```

## Tone

- Celebrate what got done, however small, without exaggerating.
- If the day went badly, say so kindly and keep the note short: "Rough day. Tomorrow
  starts with one small thing."
- Never list what they "didn't do". Unfinished things are parked, not failed.
- Not medical advice; if they say they can't switch off at night most nights, mention
  that talking to someone they trust or a professional can help.
